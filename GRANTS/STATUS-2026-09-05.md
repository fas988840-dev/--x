## Programme corrections supported by official sources

Microsoft's current programme awards credits in stages according to verified
progress and usage; neither a particular starting allowance nor acceptance
within 24–48 hours is guaranteed. Its documentation gives a typical application
review of three business days. Eligibility includes a privately held,
for-profit software company and exclusions such as consulting and crypto mining;
a wallet-data API should not be described as a mining business.
[Microsoft programme overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/startups/microsoft-for-startups/overview)

The Pyth upgrade completed on 26 August 2026. Hermes now requires an API key;
there is no demonstrated production activation here without that key and a
verified mapping. The historic grant announcement describes a **50 million
PYTH-token pool**, not $50 million or a guaranteed $1–5k developer payment.
[Pyth upgrade](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/upgrade/preparing),
[Pyth grants](https://legacy.pyth.network/grants)

Colosseum's $250,000 is selective accelerator investment. Its $25,000 Eternal
Award is separate. The current page pauses new sprints but lets already-started
sprints continue to their own deadline; a document labelled “in progress” does
not prove that an account sprint exists.
[Eternal](https://colosseum.com/eternal), [Accelerator](https://colosseum.com/accelerator)

Do not exclude Y Combinator solely for having no users or revenue: its FAQ
explicitly accepts companies at the idea stage and solo founders. This does
not imply selection, and an investment application has different terms from
cash grants or cloud credits.
[Y Combinator FAQ](https://www.ycombinator.com/faq)
